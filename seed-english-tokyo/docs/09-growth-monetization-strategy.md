# 09 · Growth & Monetization Strategy

## 1. The core tension, and how the product resolves it

Learners must never pay — the mission is free English access. Growth and revenue instead
come from making the *funding itself* the engaging, shareable, gamified layer, on top of
(not gating) the free community product. This is closer to Kickstarter/Twitch-donations
psychology than a freemium SaaS model.

## 2. Go-to-market phasing

### Phase 1 — Single-station proof (Shibuya)
Launch with one station live end-to-end (real events, real matching, real seeds) before
opening the rest of the map. A concentrated, high-density launch produces visible
momentum (a "sold out" first event, a fast-filling seedling-to-tree transition) that's
far more convincing than thin activity spread across 10 stations. Everything on `/map`
outside Shibuya displays as bare soil with a "coming next" CTA — scarcity as a feature.

### Phase 2 — Campus ambassador expansion
Once Shibuya has retained learners and a few completed events with good reviews, open the
Campus Ambassador Program at 2–3 universities within walking distance of live stations
(e.g. Waseda → Ikebukuro/Shinjuku corridor). Ambassadors are the primary organic learner
acquisition channel — students trust a peer's invite far more than an ad, and the
university leaderboard turns acquisition into a competitive, self-propagating game among
student orgs and English circles already active on campus.

### Phase 3 — Station-by-station map completion
Each new station launch is itself a marketable moment ("Shinjuku forest is growing —
plant the first seed"), giving the marketing calendar a built-in cadence of launches
instead of one big-bang release.

### Phase 4 — Supporter/diaspora funding push
Once the map has visible traction, market seed-buying to a second audience entirely:
the Japan-interested/expat/diaspora/language-enthusiast community abroad (via
travel-to-Japan and language-learning communities) who want to fund something tangible in
Tokyo without being learners themselves — this is the primary revenue growth lever, and
it only works once there's real impact data to show them (hence sequencing it after
Phases 1–3, not before).

## 3. Acquisition channels & loops

| Channel | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| Campus ambassadors | Referral codes, university leaderboard, non-cash incentives (see `06-events-matching-safety.md`) |
| Seed social sharing | Every seed purchase generates a shareable certificate/impact card (see `04-seed-tracking-system.md`) — supporters become distribution |
| Event word-of-mouth | In-person events are inherently shareable moments; photo galleries + reviews double as social proof content |
| Content/SEO | Ask English question library is public and indexable — thousands of long-tail "what's the difference between X and Y in English" queries become organic search traffic |
| Partner orgs | Language schools, international student offices, coworking spaces as event-hosting partners |

## 4. Revenue model

- **Seed tiers**: Seed ¥500 / Growth Seed ¥1,000 / Community Seed ¥5,000 / Forest Seed
  ¥10,000 — one-time by default; a recurring "monthly seed" subscription option is a
  natural V2 add (steadier revenue, same UX, just auto-replants monthly).
- **Sponsorships**: tree (station-level) and forest (city-level) sponsorships for larger
  individual or corporate donors — priced as custom pledges, not fixed tiers.
- **Corporate/CSR partnerships** (V2+): companies sponsor a station or a university
  leaderboard season in exchange for visible brand placement on `/map` and event
  materials — consistent with the transparency mission since sponsorship funding flows
  through the same public seed-attribution ledger as individual donors.
- No ads, no learner paywalls, no selling user data — monetization stays aligned with the
  mission because the only paying customers are people who explicitly want to fund
  growth, and they're rewarded with more transparency, not fewer ads.

## 5. Unit economics targets (illustrative, to refine with real data)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Cost Per Signup (CPS) | < ¥300 (should trend toward ¥0 as ambassador/organic channels mature) |
| Cost Per Learner (CPL) — signup who completes 1+ practice action | < ¥800 |
| Cost Per Attendee (CPA) — signup who checks into 1+ event | < ¥2,000 |
| Average seed size | ¥1,200 (weighted toward Growth Seed tier) |
| Supporter → repeat-supporter rate | 25%+ within 90 days (driven by impact-timeline emails: "your seed just helped someone register") |
| D30 learner retention | 35%+ (community/events-driven retention, not content-library driven) |

## 6. Retention & lifecycle marketing

- **For learners**: streak-protection nudges, weekly digest ("3 people answered your
  question," "a new event near Nakano matches your interests"), milestone achievement
  emails.
- **For supporters**: automated impact-timeline emails whenever their seed's stats move
  ("Someone in Shibuya just attended their first meetup — your seed made that possible"),
  quarterly forest-growth recap, an annual "impact report" per supporter (shareable PDF).
- **For ambassadors**: weekly leaderboard-position emails, end-of-semester campus impact
  report to show university clubs/administration for continued buy-in.

## 7. Investor narrative

"We've turned English-learning access into a transparent, trackable public good.
Every yen donated is traceable to a real person's real progress, in real time, on a map
you can watch grow. Our acquisition cost approaches zero because our two user bases —
learners and supporters — market to each other: learners generate the impact data that
makes supporters want to give more, and supporters' visible funding (new events, new
seeds appearing on the map) is itself what draws in more learners." Five-year vision:
largest English-learning community in Japan, expanding city-by-city using the same
map-and-seed mechanic (Osaka, Fukuoka, Yokohama-as-standalone, ...), with the attribution
engine and campus ambassador playbook as the reusable expansion kit.
